The Restricted Additive Schwarz (RAS) method is adapted to the
problem of computing the stationary probability distribution
vector of large, sparse, irreducible stochastic matrices.
Inexact and two-level variants are also considered, as
well as acceleration by Krylov subspace methods.
The convergence properties are analyzed and extensive
numerical experiments aimed at assessing the effect of
varying the number of subdomains and the amount of overlap
are discussed.
